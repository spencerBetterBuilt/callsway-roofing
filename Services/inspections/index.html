  • Pre-purchase inspection

What we check

Brent gets on the roof himself. It isn't a drone pass.

  • Shingles and surface

    Missing, cracked, curling, or blistering shingles; granule loss; exposed underlayment or decking.

  • Flashing and seals

    All flashing at chimneys, skylights, pipe boots, vents, and valleys, which is where most leaks start.

  • Gutters and drainage

    Gutter condition, downspout attachment, slope, and any signs of water backup or debris at the roofline.

  • Written report

    A written summary of what's good, what needs attention, and what we'd recommend doing first.

Roof inspection FAQs

Have a different question? Call Brent at (281) 221-5993.

How often should I have my roof inspected?

Most roofing professionals recommend an inspection every 2–3 years for roofs under 10 years old, and annually for roofs over 10 years old or after any significant storm. Even without obvious damage, an inspection can catch small issues like cracked shingles, failing flashing, or lifted edges before they turn into leaks.

What does a roof inspection include?

We get on the roof and inspect the shingles for wear, cracking, granule loss, and missing pieces. We check all flashing points (around chimneys, skylights, vents, and valleys), look at the gutters and fascia, and inspect the ridge and rake edges. We also check for signs of moisture from inside the attic if it's accessible. You receive a written summary of our findings.

Do I need a roof inspection before selling my home?

A pre-listing roof inspection is a smart move. It tells you what condition the roof is in before a buyer's inspector finds issues that could complicate the sale. If the roof is in good shape, you can disclose that confidently. If there are issues, you can address them on your own terms instead of during negotiations.

What if the inspection shows my roof is fine?

We tell you it's fine. If your roof has several good years left, we'll put that in writing.
