Inspection Types

The right inspection for your situation

Different situations call for different kinds of roof assessments. We offer inspections that fit your needs — whether you're buying a home, maintaining your current one, or recovering from a storm.

Pre-Purchase Inspection

Buying a home? Know exactly what you're getting into before you close. We evaluate the roof's condition, estimated remaining life, and any issues that may require near-term attention.

Annual Maintenance Inspection

Catch small problems before they become expensive ones. Annual or bi-annual inspections are the best way to extend the life of your roof and avoid surprise leaks.

Storm Damage Assessment

After hail, high winds, or a fallen tree, we document the damage thoroughly — providing the report you need to file an insurance claim or make an informed repair decision.

Pre-Listing / Seller Inspection

Selling your home? A clean roof report can be a selling point. Address issues on your timeline rather than scrambling after a buyer's inspector raises concerns.

What We Check

A thorough inspection of everything that matters

Brent personally gets on the roof — not just a drone pass — and evaluates every element that affects its performance and longevity.

  • Shingles & Surface

    Missing, cracked, curling, or blistering shingles; granule loss; exposed underlayment or decking.

  • Flashing & Seals

    Condition of all flashing at chimneys, skylights, pipe boots, vents, and valleys — the most common source of leaks.

  • Gutters & Drainage

    Gutter condition, downspout attachment, proper slope, and any signs of water backup or debris buildup at the roofline.

    You receive a written summary of findings — what's good, what needs attention, and our honest recommendation on priority and timeline.

Common Questions

Roof inspection FAQs

Answers to the questions homeowners and buyers ask us most.

How often should I have my roof inspected?

Most roofing professionals recommend an inspection every 2–3 years for roofs under 10 years old, and annually for roofs over 10 years or after any significant storm event. Even if there's no obvious damage, an inspection can catch small issues — missing or cracked shingles, failing flashing, lifted edges — before they turn into leaks and expensive repairs.

What does a roof inspection include?

We get on the roof and visually inspect the shingles for wear, cracking, granule loss, and missing pieces. We check all flashing points (around chimneys, skylights, vents, and valleys), look at the gutters and fascia, and inspect the ridge and rake edges. We also check for signs of moisture intrusion from inside the attic if accessible. You receive a written summary of our findings.

Do I need a roof inspection before selling my home?

A pre-listing roof inspection is a smart move. It tells you exactly what condition the roof is in before a buyer's inspector finds issues that could complicate the sale. If the roof is in good shape, you can disclose that confidently. If there are issues, you can address them on your own terms rather than scrambling during the negotiation period.

What if the inspection shows my roof is fine?

We tell you it's fine — and mean it. We don't manufacture problems to generate work. If your roof has several good years left, we'll put that in writing. Our business is built on honest assessments and referrals, not one-time upsells.
